Applying Calculus to Food Security

Modeling the growth rate of Malaysian Paddy (Variety MR297) using derivatives.

The Bioscience Problem

In agricultural science, predicting crop biomass over time helps optimize harvest and fertilizer schedules. Plant growth typically follows an "S-curve" or Logistic Growth Model.

As a Food Security expert analyzing MARDI's short-maturation rice varieties (like MR297, harvested at ~110-115 days), we need to know not just how big the plant is, but how fast it is growing on any given day.

*Data modeled realistically based on agronomical phenology parameters from Malaysian Agricultural Research and Development Institute (MARDI) Rice Research publications.

Mathematical Model

Total Biomass Function, $f(t)$

Measures total dry weight (grams/hill) at day $t$.

$$f(t) = \frac{L}{1 + e^{-k(t - t_0)}}$$
Crop Growth Rate (Derivative), $f'(t)$

Measures how fast biomass is added (grams/day). This is the rate of change!

$$f'(t) = \frac{L \cdot k \cdot e^{-k(t - t_0)}}{(1 + e^{-k(t - t_0)})^2}$$
  • L = 120g (Maximum biomass capacity)
  • k = 0.09 (Intrinsic growth rate constant)
  • t₀ = 55 days (Time of peak growth / Inflection point)
